How to Save Photos and Documents After Water Damage

Wet paper and photographs degrade fast, but quick steps can save a lot. Here is what to do in the first day.

By the Oxford Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-11-10

Time is the main enemy

Paper, photographs and books begin to deteriorate within hours of getting wet. Within a day or two, mold can start. Photographs may stick together, and ink may run. You often have a short window to act, and what you do in it decides what can be saved.

The first rule is to work only when it is safe to be in the area. If the water was contaminated, wear gloves and protective gear, and do not handle items that were in sewage unless the value justifies it.

Gather and sort

Move wet items to a safe, dry space. Sort by priority. Irreplaceable photographs and important legal papers come first. Everyday papers, books and magazines come next. Do not try to separate items stuck together by force.

Documents and papers

Documents that are only damp can be air dried. Lay them flat on clean paper towels or blotting paper, away from direct sun and heat. Change the blotter as it gets wet. Do not use heat or a hair dryer, which can warp paper and set ink.

For a large stack, freeze it. Place the wet papers in freezer bags with wax paper between layers where possible, and put them in a freezer. Freezing slows mold growth and gives you time. Later, thaw and air dry in small batches, or call a document restoration service for valuable items.

Photographs

Photographs are best handled while wet. Gently rinse them in clean water if they have mud or dirt, handling by the edges. Do not rub the surface. Lay them face up on clean paper towels or hang them by the corner on a line. If they are stuck together, soak in clean water and let them separate on their own.

Prints and negatives respond differently. Modern inkjet prints may run when wet. Older prints and negatives are often more resilient, but the emulsion can stick to other surfaces. When in doubt, freeze and consult a conservator.

Books

Wet books can often be saved if dried slowly. Stand them upright with the pages fanned and place paper between wet pages. Replace the paper as it absorbs water. For valuable books, freezing may be the best option until you can get help. Books damaged by contaminated water are usually not worth keeping unless sentimental.

Important documents to prioritize

Identity papers, deeds, wills, insurance policies, tax records and medical records matter most. You can often get replacement copies from issuing agencies, so do not risk your health to save paper that can be replaced. Photograph what you have so you know what was lost.

What not to do

Do not use a microwave or an oven to dry paper. Do not leave wet items in a sealed bag at room temperature, because mold can start growing. Do not use bleach on photographs or documents. Do not peel stuck pages apart dry.

Hiring a specialist

Document recovery services handle valuable or large collections. They use vacuum freeze drying and other methods that are not available at home. Ask your insurer whether the cost is covered for important items.

Think about your records

Before you move anything, photograph the room and the items. Note what is damaged. Your insurer may want a list of lost contents. Keep this list with your other claim records.

Digital copies after recovery

Once a photograph or document is dry, scan it right away. Even a damaged print can often be scanned and repaired digitally. Store the scans in two places, such as a drive at home and an online service. Label folders by year or event so the collection is easy to use.

This is also a good time to write names and dates on the backs of prints with a soft pencil, because those details are often the first thing lost in a second disaster.

Handling mud and silt

If photographs or papers came out of a flooded room with mud, do not scrape. Rinse gently in clean, cool water, holding the item by the edges. Work in a tub or sink and change the water often. Paper with silt can be dipped and drained, then dried flat. Keep your hands clean to avoid transferring contaminants, and wear gloves if the water was from a drain or a flood.

Label containers as you go so you know which items came from which room or box.

Prevention for the future

Store important documents in waterproof containers, off the floor, on a higher shelf. Scan photographs and key documents and keep a copy online or on a drive stored somewhere else. A small step now prevents a much larger loss later.

Local help in Oxford

For vital records such as birth certificates, deeds and marriage records, contact the issuing office in Lafayette County or the state to request replacements. Oxford has libraries and archives that may offer advice on preserving documents. If a loss affected your home, a restoration crew can also dry the room so you can store recovered items safely.

Questions about this topic

Should I freeze wet photographs?

Freezing is a good way to stop damage if you cannot dry them right away.

Can I dry papers in the sun?

Avoid direct sun and heat. Air dry flat in a shaded, ventilated space.

Are wet books worth saving?

Valuable or sentimental ones, yes. Most others can be replaced.

What if the paper was in sewage?

Handle only with protection. Most such items are not worth the risk unless irreplaceable.

Can documents be replaced?

Many can. Ask the issuing agency for copies.
