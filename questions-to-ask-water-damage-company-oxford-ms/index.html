Questions to Ask a Water Damage Company Before You Hire

Hiring in the middle of an emergency is hard. These questions help you compare companies quickly and spot the ones to avoid.

By the Oxford Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-11-10

Why a few questions matter

A water loss forces a quick decision about who to call. A few questions asked in the first conversation can tell you a great deal about how a company works. You do not need technical knowledge. You need clear, specific answers, and you need to notice when you do not get them.

Are you licensed and insured?

Ask what licenses the company holds for the work it will do in your area and whether it carries liability insurance. A legitimate company will say yes and will provide proof when asked. We state plainly that we are licensed and insured, and you can ask any company to show you the same. Rules for contractors can differ for different kinds of work, so ask what applies.

What does your process look like?

A good answer includes stopping the source, extraction, moisture mapping, drying with air movers and dehumidifiers, monitoring and documentation. If the answer is vague or skips moisture readings, be cautious. The water extraction page and the structural drying page describe a normal sequence.

Will you measure and record moisture?

Moisture readings are the evidence that a building is dry. Ask whether the crew will record readings at the start and during the job and give you the log. A company that dries by feel and ends by schedule can leave moisture in the walls.

What does the estimate cover?

Ask for a written scope that lists rooms, work, equipment and days. Ask what is included and what is not. Ask how the company handles extra damage found during the job. A clear estimate separates mitigation from rebuild. The key point is that a price should be explainable line by line.

Do you work with insurance?

A company can document and share records with your insurer at your request. It cannot decide coverage. Be careful of anyone who promises what your insurer will pay or asks you to sign over your claim rights before explaining what that means. Ask what documentation you will receive: photos, readings and an itemized scope.

What happens if the work is not right?

Ask how the company handles problems. A reputable company has a process for return visits and for correcting work. Ask for it in writing where appropriate.

Who will be at my home?

Ask whether the crew is employees or subcontractors and who is responsible for the job. Ask how you will reach someone during the work.

Warning signs

Avoid companies that demand full payment up front, pressure you to sign immediately, refuse to explain the scope or knock on doors after a storm. Be cautious of promises that sound too good, such as a fixed arrival time during a storm. Nobody can promise that when roads, power and call volume are unpredictable.

Ask about the emergency line

Ask whether the phone is answered at all hours and who answers it. A 24/7 line means someone is available at night and on weekends. Ask what to do while you wait, and listen for safety steps like shutting off the water and staying away from electrical hazards.

Compare scopes, not only totals

Two estimates with the same total can cover very different work. Ask each company to explain the differences. The cheapest option that leaves out drying readings or removes less material may cost you more later.

What to have ready when you call

A company can help faster if you can answer a few questions. Where did the water start? When did you notice it? Has the source been stopped? What rooms are wet? Is there any chance the water came from a drain or from outside? Do you have photos? Having these answers ready also shows you how the company responds, because a good dispatcher will ask them.

Questions about timing

Ask when the crew can start, how long they expect to be on site each day and how often they will return for readings. Honest answers include uncertainty, because weather, access and the size of the loss change the plan. A company that sounds certain about everything before seeing your home is selling, not planning.

Trust your sense of the conversation

A good company answers questions without irritation, explains in plain words and does not rush you past something you do not understand.

Checking local requirements in Oxford

For questions about permits on rebuilding work in Oxford, the City of Oxford Building Department at 107 Courthouse Square is the right place to ask. Outside the city, in the rest of Lafayette County, rules differ. Ask any company which permits, if any, apply to your job and who will handle them.

Questions about this topic

Should I get several estimates?

If water is not actively spreading, comparing two or three scopes is reasonable. Stop the source first.

Is a deposit normal?

Terms vary. Ask for them in writing and avoid paying in full before work begins.

Can the company talk to my insurer?

It can share documents at your request. The insurer decides coverage.

What if the company wants me to sign right away?

Ask to take the paperwork and read it. A reputable company allows that.

How do I check a company's insurance?

Ask for a certificate of insurance and confirm it with the insurer if you wish.
