Hardwood Floor Water Damage Repair in Oxford, MS

Cupped, crowned or buckled boards mean moisture got under the finish. Dry them quickly and decide what to repair or replace.

☎ Call (662) 757-1011

Hardwood floor water damage repair in Oxford, MS depends on timing and on the cause. Wood absorbs water and swells, and the first sign is usually cupping, where the edges of the boards rise higher than the centers. Fast action and careful drying can save a floor that looks ruined, and the wrong move, such as sanding too soon, can ruin one that would have recovered.

Hardwood flooring is normally installed at about 6 to 9 percent moisture content. Water pushes it much higher, and the boards expand against each other. People describe this as walnut flooring that has started to cup, a washer valve that broke and went unnoticed for an hour, or a condensate line that backed up beside the floor.

What cupping, crowning and buckling mean

Cupping happens when the bottom of a board is wetter than the top. The edges curl up. Crowning is the opposite, and it can appear after a cupped floor is sanded flat while still wet. Buckling is more serious. Boards lift off the subfloor and may separate from it. Staining, dark spots and gaps between boards show that water has been present for some time.

Cupping is often reversible if the floor dries gradually and the finish is intact. Buckling often means replacement of the affected boards, and the floor under them must be checked.

Hardwood floor emergency: the first steps

1. Stop the water source. Close the supply valve for the appliance or the main valve.

2. Remove standing water and wipe up what you can with towels.

3. Remove rugs, furniture and anything resting on the wet floor.

4. Do not sand, seal or refinish until the floor is dry.

5. Photograph the floor and call for a moisture reading.

Why hardwood needs special drying

Wood dries from the surface, but moisture is also under it, in the subfloor and in the air gap below. A dehumidifier and a few fans will dry the top of the boards and leave the underside wet. Specialized floor drying systems pull moisture through the boards with controlled suction under mats, or push air under the floor through small ports.

Fast drying can crack a floor, so drying is controlled and monitored.

The crew tracks readings every day. When the boards return to a level close to their normal range, a floor that has cupped may flatten on its own over several weeks. Sanding is a later decision, after the floor has settled.

Laminate, engineered wood and solid hardwood

Solid hardwood can often be dried and refinished. Engineered wood has a thin top layer over plywood, which can delaminate when wet. Laminate has a fiberboard core that swells and does not recover when it is soaked. A moisture reading and a close look at the board edge tell the crew which type is present, and which one is salvageable.

The kitchen and appliance link

Questions Oxford homeowners ask

Can a cupped hardwood floor be saved?

Often, if the finish is intact and the floor is dried gradually. A moisture reading shows how far the floor has recovered before anyone sands it.

Should I sand right away?

No. Sanding a wet floor can lead to crowning later. Wait until the moisture readings are stable.

How long does hardwood take to dry?

Often several days to a few weeks, depending on the species, thickness and amount of water. Readings decide, not the calendar.

Is laminate or engineered flooring different?

Yes. Laminate with a fiberboard core rarely recovers. Engineered wood depends on the thickness of the top layer.

Does homeowners insurance cover floor damage?

It depends on the cause. Sudden leaks are often covered and gradual leaks often are not. Ask your insurer.

Ask about the industry standard

Professional water damage work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. When you compare companies for hardwood floor water damage work, ask whether the crew works to it and how moisture readings will be recorded.
