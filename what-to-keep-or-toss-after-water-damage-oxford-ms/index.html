What to Keep or Toss After Water Damage

Deciding what to save is the hardest part of cleanup. Water type, time and material give a clear rule for most items.

By the Oxford Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-11-10

Three questions decide almost everything

After a water loss, every item in the room seems to demand a decision. Three questions simplify them. What kind of water touched it? How long was it wet? Is the material porous or hard?

Clean water that was removed fast leaves many things recoverable. Contaminated water, long contact time and porous materials point to discarding. The industry uses the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard to guide these calls, and the categories are explained in the water damage industry's usual terms: clean, gray and black.

Start with safety

Before touching anything, make sure the electricity is safe and the source is stopped. Wear gloves and boots if the water came from a drain or outside. Do not eat food that touched floodwater, and do not use appliances that were submerged until they are checked.

Hard and non-porous items

Glass, metal, plastic, ceramic and sealed stone can usually be cleaned and disinfected, even after contaminated water. Wash with detergent, rinse and disinfect according to the product directions. Dry thoroughly before storing.

Porous items

Carpet, pad, upholstered furniture, mattresses, particleboard, fiberboard and insulation absorb water. After clean water and quick drying, some are salvageable. After gray or black water, discard them. See the carpet page for the details on flooring.

Clothing and fabric

Washable fabrics can be laundered, even after contaminated water, in hot water with detergent and disinfectant if appropriate. Dry cleanable items go to a cleaner who handles water damage. Items with mold or lingering odor should be replaced.

Paper, books and photos

These are time sensitive. Paper that sits wet for more than a day or two can begin to grow mold. Freeze wet documents in a freezer bag to stop deterioration until you can dry them. The next guide on saving documents covers this in more detail, but the main rule is to act quickly.

Food and kitchen

Discard food in cans that touched floodwater unless cleaned and disinfected, and discard any open packaged food that was wet. Throw out food from a refrigerator that lost power for long enough to spoil. When in doubt, throw it out.

Electronics and appliances

Do not plug in electronics that got wet. Have appliances checked by a technician before use. Small electronics that were submerged in contaminated water are usually lost.

Building materials

Drywall that is soft, crumbly or discolored is removed. Insulation that was wet is usually replaced. Baseboards and trim may dry in place. Flooring depends on type, as explained on the hardwood floor page. Subfloor is checked with a meter.

Working through a room in order

Work from the driest areas to the wettest, and from hard items to soft ones. Take hard goods outdoors to wash and disinfect. Bag porous items that must go, and label the bags so you can match them to your list. Leave the structure to the crew, because wet walls and floors need equipment, not scrubbing. Finish by wiping surfaces that were splashed, even if they look clean.

Take breaks, and ask for help lifting heavy furniture. Wet carpet and pad are much heavier than they look.

Cleaning and disinfecting what you keep

Wash hard items with hot water and detergent first, because disinfectant does not work well on dirt. Rinse, apply a household disinfectant according to the label, let it sit for the stated contact time and rinse again where the label says to. Never mix cleaning products, and ventilate the room. Let items air dry completely before storing them in a closed box.

For wood furniture, wipe dry, let it dry slowly away from heat, and check joints and veneers for lifting over the next weeks.

Keep a running list

As you decide, write each item on a list with a rough age and value and whether it was kept, cleaned or discarded. A list built as you work is far more accurate than one built from memory a month later, and it speeds up any contents claim.

Photograph before you discard

Photograph the items, the damage and the date. List major items, with their approximate value, to support an insurance claim. Ask your insurer how long to keep discarded items, or whether it wants to see them first.

Sentimental items

Some belongings matter more than their price. Set them aside and ask a restoration specialist for photographs, textiles or artwork. Not everything can be saved, but quick action helps.

When in doubt

Set aside questionable items in a dry place and make the decision after the room is dried. Do not hurry to discard what could be documented, and do not keep what is contaminated.

Disposal and cleanup in Oxford

Large amounts of discarded carpet, drywall and furniture are bulky and heavy. Ask the City of Oxford about its rules for bulk trash and disposal. For Lafayette County residents outside the city, check with county services. Photograph items before they leave the house so your records are complete.

Questions about this topic

Can I keep a couch that got wet?

If clean water and quick drying, sometimes. If contaminated or long wet, discard it.

Is it safe to keep food that got wet?

Discard food that touched floodwater or contaminated water. When in doubt, throw it out.

Should I keep damaged items for the insurer?

Photograph them and ask the insurer before you discard anything of value.

Can books be saved?

Sometimes, if frozen or dried quickly. Mold can begin to grow within days.

What about appliances?

Do not plug them in. Have a technician check them.
