DIY Drying vs Professional Help After a Leak

Some small spills are fine to handle yourself. Others look small and are not. Here is how to tell which is which.

By the Oxford Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-11-10

Where DIY works

A small amount of clean water on a hard floor, caught right away, is a job for towels and a fan. A spilled pot or an overflowed sink that never reached the walls can be cleaned up without a crew. If you can wipe up all of the water, the materials are non-porous, and everything feels dry within a few hours, DIY is a reasonable choice.

The trouble is that a small spill and the start of a large loss look the same in the first minute. The questions below help you decide.

Four questions to ask

Where did the water come from? Clean supply water is far safer to handle than water from a drain, appliance discharge or outside.

How much is there? Standing water that covers a floor or has soaked carpet is more than a spill.

How long has it been there? Water that sat overnight has had time to move into walls and flooring.

What did it touch? Water that reached drywall, insulation, carpet pad or hardwood will not dry with a fan.

Limits of household equipment

Household fans move air but do not remove moisture from it. A bathroom fan or a single box fan can dry a surface and leave the inside wet. A consumer dehumidifier handles a small area but is not sized for a flooded room. Wet vacuums remove surface water but they leave water soaked into the pad and subfloor.

Professional equipment is designed to dry whole rooms and wall cavities. It also comes with meters that tell you when the job is done. That is the part DIY lacks: without a reading, you are guessing.

What DIY can do while you wait

Even if you plan to call a crew, there are useful steps.

  1. Stop the water source and close the valve.
  2. Remove standing water with towels or a wet vacuum if the area is dry of electrical hazards.
  3. Move furniture and belongings off wet surfaces.
  4. Lift rugs and place foil or plastic under furniture legs.
  5. Photograph everything for your records.
  6. Run fans and a dehumidifier only to keep air moving, not as a replacement for drying.

When to call a professional

Call when water has reached a wall, a ceiling or flooring, when it came from a drain or outside, when more than a small area is wet, when you smell mustiness or when you cannot find the source. Also call when you are unsure. A moisture reading can answer the question quickly. The structural drying page explains the equipment crews use.

Safety first

Do not touch electrical equipment standing in water. Turn off power to the affected room only if you can reach the panel while dry. Do not enter a ceiling or crawl space with standing water. Keep children and pets out of the area.

Cost of waiting

The cost of not drying properly is often larger than the cost of professional drying. Moisture trapped in walls can lead to odor, warped flooring and mold growth, and the repair after the fact usually costs more than the first job would have. This is why a quick moisture check is worth it even if you plan to do most of the work yourself.

A middle path

You can combine the two. A crew can extract water and set up equipment, and you can handle moving belongings, photographs and trips to the store. Ask what the crew would like you to do and not to do.

A word about renting equipment

Equipment rental stores carry air movers and dehumidifiers. They can work for small jobs if you know how to place them and how to tell when to stop. The risk is the same as with any DIY drying. Without meter readings, you cannot know whether the walls are dry.

If you rent equipment, ask the store how many units suit the size of the area, and keep the room closed so the dehumidifier can work.

Signs your DIY drying is not working

The room still smells musty after a few days. Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall. Paint is bubbling. The floor feels cool and damp. The dehumidifier fills again and again with no change. Any of these means moisture is still in the structure, and it is time to bring in meters and stronger equipment.

Insurance considerations

Insurers expect you to take reasonable steps to limit damage. Document what you did. If you used your own equipment, keep receipts. Ask whether the cost of renting equipment is covered.

Drying in a humid climate

North Mississippi summers are humid, which makes drying harder, and a house that is closed up with the air conditioner running may still hold moisture in walls and flooring. If you are drying part of a house yourself, watch for odors and for paint that bubbles, and ask a crew for readings. For permit questions about rebuilding, contact the City of Oxford Building Department at 107 Courthouse Square.

Questions about this topic

Can I dry a wet carpet myself?

If the water was clean and the area small, extraction and fans can work. The pad often needs to come out.

Is a household dehumidifier enough?

For small areas and light dampness, sometimes. For a flooded room, no.

How do I know if the wall is dry?

You cannot tell by touch. A moisture meter gives a reading.

Should I leave fans on all night?

Fans help move air. They do not remove moisture without a dehumidifier.

What if I am not sure?

Call and describe what happened. A dispatcher can say whether a visit makes sense.
