Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Oxford, MS

Wet cabinets, a swollen toe-kick or a soft floor under the sink all point to a hidden leak. Dry, repair and rebuild the kitchen.

☎ Call (662) 757-1011

A kitchen takes water from more directions than any other room, and kitchen water damage restoration in Oxford, MS has to account for all of them. The sink, the dishwasher, the refrigerator ice maker and the plumbing in the wall all share one small space.

When something leaks, cabinets, flooring and drywall are wet together, and the first step is closing the shut-off valve under the sink or behind the appliance.

Kitchen cabinets are the central problem. Most are built from particle board or medium density fiberboard, which swells and crumbles when it stays wet. Solid wood cabinets tolerate water better, but water trapped behind them still damages drywall and subfloor.

A call from someone with a burst pipe under a sink and water all over the kitchen floor is the extreme case, but a slow drip under the sink does the same damage over weeks.

Signs of water damage in a kitchen

- A swollen or peeling toe-kick

- A soft or spongy spot on the floor near the sink or dishwasher

- Cabinet doors that no longer close flat

- A musty smell inside the sink cabinet

- A hole forming in the wall near a pipe, or a bulge in the drywall

- Water stains on the underside of the countertop

Under-sink leaks and wet cabinets

Under-sink leaks usually come from a drain trap, a supply connection or a garbage disposal. The water runs down the inside of the cabinet and spreads across the base. A cabinet floor that has been wet for a week is already swollen. The toe-kick, the base plate and the subfloor beneath it need checking.

If the cabinet bottom is intact, a crew can often dry it in place with small drying equipment. If the particle board has swelled or crumbled, it has to be replaced. Replacing a cabinet base can mean removing the countertop, so that decision belongs in the scope before the drying starts.

What a crew does in a kitchen

The crew first closes or confirms the source is closed, then extracts standing water. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where water moved behind cabinets, under the floor and into the wall. Toe-kicks and base trim are removed so air can flow. A small hole may be drilled in the cabinet back or toe-kick to push air into the cavity, which dries without demolition when the materials are salvageable.

Floors are the second question. Tile can survive if the substrate is sound. Vinyl and laminate often trap water underneath. Hardwood may be dried with specialized mats. The hardwood floor page explains that process in detail.

What affects the cost of kitchen restoration

COST

Cabinet condition :: Swollen particle board must be replaced, and that often means removing countertops.

Flooring :: Under-cabinet flooring is the hardest to dry and the most expensive to replace.

Wall damage :: Water that reached the wall behind a sink adds drywall and trim work.

Appliances :: Moving a refrigerator or range to reach the floor adds labor.

Drying days :: Enclosed cabinet cavities may need extra days.

You will hear the scope and price before work begins. Sudden failures such as a burst supply line are often covered by homeowners insurance, while a slow drip over weeks may not be. Ask your insurer and photograph the cabinets, the floor and the source before anything is removed.

Questions Oxford homeowners ask

Do you offer a kitchen water damage inspection at any hour?

The dispatch line is answered 24/7. We offer same-day response when crews are available. For slow leaks, an inspection can be scheduled the same day or the next, depending on the job.

Can wet cabinets be saved?

Solid wood often can, and particle board often cannot. A crew checks with moisture meters and tells you what is salvageable before removal.

Do countertops have to come off?

Only if the cabinet base has to be replaced. A dry cabinet with a wet toe-kick usually stays.

How long does a kitchen take to dry?

Several days, depending on the materials and the amount of water. Enclosed cabinets can add days.

Is kitchen water damage covered by insurance?

Sudden leaks are often covered, and long-term leaks often are not. Ask your insurer and keep the failed part.

Ask about the industry standard

Professional water damage work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. When you compare companies for kitchen water damage work, ask whether the crew works to it and how moisture readings will be recorded.
