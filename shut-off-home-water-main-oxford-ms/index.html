How to Shut Off Your Home Water Main

When a pipe breaks, the next minute matters more than the next hour. Learn where your shutoffs are before you need them.

By the Oxford Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-11-10

Why this is the first thing to know

Water damage grows with every minute the water runs. A supply line can release many gallons while you look for a phone number. The single most useful thing a homeowner can do is know where the shutoff valves are and practice closing them once.

There are two kinds of valves to find. Fixture valves serve one toilet, sink or appliance. The main valve serves the whole house. Closing a fixture valve stops one leak and leaves the rest of the house with water. Closing the main stops everything.

Fixture valves

Look under sinks, behind toilets and behind washing machines. A small oval or round handle on the supply line is the fixture valve. Turn it clockwise until it stops. Older valves can be stiff or corroded, so turn gently. If a valve breaks or will not move, go to the main.

Water heaters have their own cold water valve on the inlet pipe. Closing it stops water from filling a leaking tank, and the water heater leak page explains the rest of the steps.

The main valve inside the house

In many homes the main valve is where the water line enters the house. Look in a utility room, a garage, a basement, a crawl space or a closet near the water heater. It may be a wheel handle, called a gate valve, or a lever handle, called a ball valve. A wheel turns clockwise to close. A lever closes when it is perpendicular to the pipe.

The meter box and street valve

Outside, most properties have a meter box set in the ground near the street or the front of the lot. It has a lid, often labeled for water. Inside you may find the meter and a shutoff valve on the house side. The utility valve on the street side belongs to the utility and is not for homeowners to operate.

Use the valve on your side of the meter when there is one, and call the utility if you cannot reach it.

What to do after the water is off

1. Open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain the remaining water.

2. Turn off the water heater power or gas if the heater may run dry. Electric elements can burn out without water.

3. Move belongings away from the wet area.

4. Photograph the damage and the source.

5. Call a plumber for the repair and a restoration crew for the water.

If water has reached outlets or the panel, do not touch the panel. Call the power company or 911 first.

If you cannot find or close the main

Call the dispatch line anyway. A dispatcher can ask where your water enters and which direction the pipe runs, and help you find the valve. If it cannot be closed, call Oxford Utilities and ask about the meter shutoff. A plumber can also replace a stuck valve afterward, which is a worthwhile repair because a valve you cannot close is a risk.

Special cases

Some homes have more than one main shutoff. A house with an addition, a detached garage or an irrigation system may have separate lines, each with its own valve. A sprinkler line that breaks outside can still send water toward the foundation, so find the irrigation shutoff too.

In multi-family buildings, the shutoff for one unit may be shared, so ask the landlord or property manager where it is before an emergency.

Practice once

Close the main and open a faucet to confirm the water stops. Open it again and check for leaks around the valve. Tag it with a bright label so the next person in the house can find it. Do this twice a year if you can, because valves that are never turned tend to seize.

Preventing the next emergency

Replace braided supply hoses on washers every few years and check for bulges. Look under sinks twice a year. If you travel, see whether a smart shutoff or a leak sensor makes sense. These small steps reduce the size of a loss more than any restoration crew can after the fact.

Water service in Oxford

Oxford Utilities provides water service in the city, and the meter and the lines on the street side belong to the utility. If you cannot close a valve on your side of the meter, call the utility and ask what it can do. A sudden jump in your water bill can also point to a hidden leak, so ask Oxford Utilities to check your usage if you suspect one.

Questions about this topic

Where is the main water valve usually located?

Often where the water line enters the house, in a utility area, garage, crawl space or near the water heater. There may also be a shutoff at the meter box.

Can I use the valve at the street?

The valve on the street side of the meter belongs to the utility. Use the valve on your side, or call the utility.

Which way do I turn the valve?

Wheel handles close clockwise. Lever handles close when they are crosswise to the pipe.

Should I turn off the water heater too?

If the heater may run dry, turn off its power or gas to protect it. A dispatcher can advise.

What if the valve will not move?

Do not force it hard. Call the utility or a plumber and call us for the cleanup.
