How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your House

Some leaks never drip where you can see them. These checks narrow down where water is going before it damages the structure.

By the Oxford Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-11-10

Why hidden leaks are costly

A leak you can see is a leak you can stop. A leak inside a wall, under a slab or above a ceiling can run for weeks. By the time a stain shows up, the damage may already include wet insulation, soft drywall and warped flooring. Finding the leak early is the cheapest repair you can make.

The following checks cost little and need no special equipment. If they point to a leak, a plumber can locate and repair it, and a restoration crew can dry what got wet.

Signs that suggest a hidden leak

- A water bill that rises without a change in how you use water

- The sound of running water when everything is off

- A musty smell in a room or closet

- Warm or damp spots on a floor

- Paint that bubbles or peels without a clear reason

- Cracks in walls or floors that seem to be growing

- Low water pressure in one part of the house

One sign is a clue. Two or three together are a stronger reason to investigate.

The water meter test

This is the most reliable do-it-yourself check. Turn off every faucet and appliance that uses water. Look at your meter. Many meters have a small dial or triangle that spins when water moves. If it moves with everything off, water is leaking somewhere after the meter. Note the reading, wait an hour without using water and read it again. A change confirms a leak.

If the meter does not move, the leak may be intermittent, or the problem may be somewhere else, such as a roof or an air conditioner.

Isolate the leak

Close the main valve and watch the meter. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house. Then close individual fixture valves one at a time. When the meter stops, you have found the line.

Check the usual suspects

Look under every sink and behind every toilet and appliance. Check the water heater base, the ice maker line and the washer hoses. Look at the ceiling beneath upstairs bathrooms. Check the toilet for a silent leak by adding a few drops of food coloring to the tank, waiting without flushing, and seeing whether color appears in the bowl.

Slab leaks and underground lines

In homes built on a slab, supply lines can run under the concrete. Signs include a warm spot on a floor, the sound of water under the floor and a meter that moves with everything off. The slab leak page explains what a leak under concrete does. Irrigation lines and the line from the meter to the house can also leak underground.

Soggy ground, a patch of unusually green grass or a sinkhole may point to one.

Appliance and air conditioner sources

A washing machine hose, a dishwasher line and a refrigerator ice maker line all leak quietly. The appliance leak page lists the common failure points. A clogged air conditioner drain pan can overflow into a ceiling or closet.

Use a moisture meter or thermal camera

Professionals use moisture meters and thermal cameras to find water inside walls without cutting. Pinpointing the source before opening drywall saves repair work. The meters help show where water traveled as well as where it started, because water often runs along framing before it shows.

When the leak is outside the walls

Not every hidden leak is a pipe. Water can enter from a failed window seal, a roof penetration or a foundation crack, and it can then travel along framing to show up far from the entry point. If the meter test is clean and you still see signs, think about rain. Does the stain grow after storms?

A roofer or a restoration crew can trace the path with a moisture meter.

Keep a simple log of when you notice signs and what the weather and water use were that day. Patterns make a source much easier to find.

What to do when you find it

Close the valve that serves the leak. Call a plumber to repair the line. Call a restoration crew to dry the wet materials. Take photos and note the date for your records. Ask your insurer about coverage before work starts, because long-term leaks are often treated differently from sudden ones.

Prevention

Replace old washer hoses, check under sinks twice a year and watch your water bill for changes. A small leak sensor placed under a sink or beside a water heater is an inexpensive early warning.

Checking usage with Oxford Utilities

If you suspect a hidden leak in Oxford, Oxford Utilities can help you compare your recent water usage and can tell you whether the meter shows continuous flow. For plumbing repairs that open walls or floors, ask the City of Oxford Building Department at 107 Courthouse Square whether a permit applies.

Questions about this topic

How do I know if my meter shows a leak?

Turn off all water use and check whether the meter still moves. Movement means water is going somewhere.

Can a leak be hidden even if my bill is normal?

Yes. A small or intermittent leak may not change the bill much.

How do I find a leak under a slab?

Warm floor spots, the sound of water and a moving meter are signs. A plumber uses specialized tools to pinpoint it.

Is a toilet leak a real cost?

A silent toilet leak can waste a lot of water over time. The dye test is a quick check.

Who do I call first?

A plumber repairs the pipe, and a restoration crew dries the damage. Call both if the structure is wet.
