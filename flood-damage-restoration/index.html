Emergency Flood Damage Cleanup in Oxford, MS

River, creek and runoff flooding brings contaminated water that needs careful removal. Emergency cleanup, drying and documentation, 24/7.

Floodwater is an emergency that cannot wait for morning. Emergency flood damage cleanup in Oxford, MS begins with a call to our 24/7 line, and the first rule is safety. Do not wade through standing water if electrical outlets, appliances or the panel may be involved, and do not assume the water is clean.

Flood damage is the result of rising water from a river, a creek, heavy rain or surface runoff. It is different from a burst pipe because floodwater carries soil, bacteria and debris. The industry sorts water into three categories, and floodwater is treated as Category 3, the most contaminated, until proven otherwise. That changes what can be saved.

When flooding is an emergency in Oxford

Flooding has reached Lafayette County homes before. Heavy rain in February 2019 pushed the Yocona River past flood stage, and county emergency management reported floodwater damage to houses and businesses. On April 5, 2025, storms brought street flooding to Oxford and flash flooding on several roads. The April 2025 event did not need a river at all, because runoff and overwhelmed storm drains were enough.

If water is entering your home, do these things in order:

1. Get everyone out of the water and away from electrical equipment.

2. Call the utility or 911 if you see sparks or smell gas.

3. Take photos from a dry spot and note the high-water line on walls.

4. Call the 24/7 line and ask about same-day response.

5. Do not run a household vacuum or open the breaker panel if it is wet.

A same-day slot is offered when one is open, and after-hours calls are normal in flood events.

What makes floodwater different

Floodwater is gray or black water. Porous materials that it touches, such as carpet, carpet pad, insulation and drywall, usually cannot be cleaned and are removed. Hard materials such as concrete, tile and framing can be cleaned and disinfected. Contents such as mattresses, upholstered furniture and particle board furniture are generally discarded.

This is why flood cleanup costs more than clean-water cleanup of the same area. It is also why speed matters. Wet materials left in place begin to break down and grow microbes quickly.

How the crew approaches a flood loss

The crew wears protective gear and starts by pumping out standing water in stages. Extraction units clear what pumps cannot reach. Affected drywall is cut out above the high-water line, and carpet, pad and insulation are removed and bagged. Tile, concrete and framing get a thorough cleaning and a treatment pass. Then air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure while moisture meters track progress.

Contents are sorted into what can be cleaned, what needs specialist help, and what should be discarded. Document everything before it leaves the house.

Flood insurance versus homeowners insurance

Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood damage from rising water. A separate flood policy is what typically responds, and flood policies often have waiting periods before they begin. Read your policy, ask your insurer what applies, and check the FEMA flood map for your address to see whether your property is in a mapped flood area.

Questions Oxford homeowners ask

Do you handle flood emergencies at night or on weekends in Oxford?

Yes. The 24/7 line is answered overnight and on weekends, which is when most storm flooding happens. We offer same-day response when crews are available, and we do not promise an arrival time because road conditions and call volume change during storms.

Is floodwater really that dangerous?

It can be. Floodwater may carry bacteria from drains and soil, and it often hides electrical hazards. Treat it as contaminated until a crew tells you otherwise.

Can I clean up floodwater myself?

Small amounts of clean runoff in a garage, yes. Anything that came from outside, a drain or a toilet is better handled by a crew with protective gear and disinfectants.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

Usually not for rising water. A separate flood policy typically covers it, and some policies have waiting periods. Ask your insurer before assuming.

How do I know if my property is in a flood zone?

Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask the City of Oxford Building Department whether the property is in a mapped floodplain.

Ask about the industry standard

Professional water damage work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. When you compare companies for flood damage cleanup work, ask whether the crew works to it and how moisture readings will be recorded.
