Emergency storm water damage cleanup in Oxford, MS starts with protecting people, then belongings, then the structure. Call our 24/7 line, photograph the damage from a safe spot, and tell the dispatcher where the water entered.

Storm water damage is wind, rain and the openings they create. It is different from flood damage, where water rises from the ground, because storm water comes through the building envelope from above or from the side. A plain roof leak with no storm belongs on the ceiling water damage page, and rising water belongs on the flood damage page.

This page covers the damage that wind and rain do together.

Storm water emergency: what to do first

1. Check that people and pets are safe and keep away from downed power lines.

2. Move furniture, electronics and documents away from wet areas.

3. Place buckets under drips and put towels along window sills.

4. Take photos and short video of the damage before anything is moved.

5. Call the 24/7 line and ask about same-day response.

Storms arrive at night and on weekends, so dispatch is open at those hours. A crew can often come the same day, depending on availability, though widespread storms mean many calls at once.

What storms do to a building

Wind lifts shingles and flashing, and rain follows. Gutters overflow and send water down walls. Windows and doors leak at seals under driving rain. Siding allows water behind it and into wall cavities, where it can run unseen for days. Fallen limbs puncture roofs. Attics that were dry for years show their first stain.

Oxford saw this on April 5, 2025. Tornado sirens sounded for more than 90 minutes, heavy rain followed, and the city had street flooding and flash flooding on several roads. In the eastern part of the county, at least 17 homes were reported damaged, mostly by wind.

Temporary protection

A tarp over a damaged roof, plywood over a broken window and boards over an open wall all limit further damage. Temporary protection is not a repair, but insurers generally expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more loss, so keep receipts. Only attempt this from the ground or a stable surface. Never climb a wet roof.

How a crew handles storm water damage

The crew checks for the entry point and for how far the water traveled. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show wet insulation, drywall and framing. Wet insulation and drywall are removed, and air movers and dehumidifiers dry the rest. A roofer or contractor repairs the opening. We coordinate so drying starts as soon as the building is closed in.

Questions Oxford homeowners ask

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Oxford?

Yes. The 24/7 line is answered overnight and on weekends. Storms create heavy call volume, so we do not promise an arrival time, but we offer same-day response when crews are available.

Should I tarp my roof myself?

Only from a safe position and never on a wet roof. A roofer can do it safely. Keep receipts for any temporary protection.

Does homeowners insurance cover storm water damage?

Wind and rain damage is often covered, but flood damage usually is not. Ask your insurer.

Can storm damage show up later?

Yes. Water in attics and wall cavities can show up days later as stains, odors or damp baseboards.

Is storm damage the same as flood damage?

No. Storm damage comes through the building envelope. Flood damage is rising water from the ground. The insurance treatment often differs.

Ask about the industry standard

Professional water damage work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. When you compare companies for storm water damage work, ask whether the crew works to it and how moisture readings will be recorded.

After a storm, what to read next

A leak that shows up as a stain overhead is covered under ceiling water damage repair, and water that rose from the ground is covered under flood damage cleanup. Water in a lower level is covered under basement water damage, and the drying stage is explained under structural drying.
