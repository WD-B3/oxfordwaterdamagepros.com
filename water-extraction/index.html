Water extraction means physically removing liquid water from floors, carpet and low spaces with pumps and extraction units, before drying equipment goes in. Extraction clears the bulk of the water in hours. Drying then removes the moisture left inside materials over the following days.

When standing water is an emergency

Not every puddle needs a crew, but several situations do. One inch of water across 1,000 square feet is about 623 gallons, which is more than most people can mop. Water that has reached carpet pad, a wall base or an interior door threshold is already moving into materials.

Water that came from a toilet, a floor drain or outside the house should be treated as contaminated until a crew says otherwise.

Call right away if any of these apply:

- Water is deeper than a shallow film across a whole room

- It has soaked carpet, rugs or hardwood

- It is near electrical outlets, a panel or appliances

- It came from a sewer line, a toilet or a flooded yard

- It is spreading into a second room or a lower floor

After hours and weekends are when most of these calls arrive, so dispatch is staffed overnight. A crew can often come the same day, depending on availability, and the dispatcher will tell you honestly what to expect.

How extraction works

The crew starts by finding and confirming the source is closed. Then the equipment goes in. A submersible pump handles deep water. A truck-mounted or portable extraction unit with a wand pulls water from carpet and from hard floors. Squeegee tools and a wet vacuum work the edges and corners that the larger units cannot reach.

Wet carpet pad is usually lifted and removed, because it holds water like a sponge and dries slowly. The carpet itself can sometimes be floated, meaning it is peeled back from the tack strip, supported off the floor and dried in place. Whether that works depends on how long the water sat and what kind of water it was.

What can be saved

Clean water caught early gives the most options. Hardwood can often be dried with extraction and specialized mats if the boards have not cupped. Drywall that was only wetted at the base can sometimes be dried in place. Water that has been sitting for days, or that was contaminated, takes more materials out of the equation.

Where extraction ends and drying begins

A floor that looks dry after extraction is not dry. Water remains in baseboards, the pad, the subfloor and the wall cavity. That is why extraction is followed by structural drying and dehumidification, where air movers and dehumidifiers run for days while moisture meters track the readings. Skipping that step is how a one-day cleanup turns into a musty-smelling floor a month later.

Questions Oxford homeowners ask

Do you handle water extraction emergencies at night or on weekends in Oxford?

Yes. The dispatch line is answered 24 hours a day and extraction crews work after hours. The dispatcher tells you what to shut off while a crew is arranged, and same-day visits are offered when a crew is free. We do not promise an arrival time because storms and call volume change what is possible.

Can I use a shop vacuum to remove standing water myself?

For a small clean spill, a wet and dry shop vacuum can work. For anything deeper, or anything near outlets, a household vacuum is a shock risk and a shop vacuum may not keep up. If more than a thin film of water is involved, call before you plug anything in.

How long does water extraction take?

Removing standing water usually takes hours rather than days, depending on depth and area. The drying phase that follows takes several days and is tracked with moisture readings.

What is the difference between water extraction and water removal?

They describe the same first step, taking liquid water out of the building. Extraction is the trade term for doing it with pumps and extraction units. Water removal is the phrase most homeowners search for.

Will extraction save my carpet?

Sometimes. Clean water caught within a day or so gives carpet the best chance, usually with the pad removed. Water that has sat longer or was contaminated generally means replacing the carpet.

Ask about the industry standard

Professional water damage work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. When you compare companies for water extraction work, ask whether the crew works to it and how moisture readings will be recorded.
