Water Damage Restoration in the North and South Lamar Historic Districts, Oxford, MS

Older homes in Oxford's local historic districts raise questions that newer houses do not. Here is how water damage is handled and who to ask about repairs.

☎ Call (662) 757-1011

About the districts

Oxford has local historic districts overseen by its Historic Preservation Commission. In 2021 the city reduced them from five to three: North Lamar, Courthouse Square and South Lamar. Oxford Eagle coverage said the revised districts include 642 properties, after about 62 were removed, most of them multi-unit properties on the edges. This page covers the North and South Lamar districts.

The city's draft interactive map shows the exact boundaries, so check it before assuming a street is inside.

Drying an older house

Older houses are built differently from newer ones, with materials that vary from home to home. Nothing here says they leak more or suffer more damage. The point is that a crew should look before cutting. Original plaster, wood trim and old floors may be worth saving, and the right drying plan balances speed against the risk of damaging them.

- Stains and sags in plaster or drywall ceilings, covered under ceiling water damage repair

- Wet walls and trim, covered under wall and drywall repair

- Wood floors that cup or stain, covered under hardwood floor repair

- Storm water that enters through a roof or window, covered under storm water damage cleanup

Preservation questions

Historic districts have design review for certain exterior changes. The City of Oxford publishes Historic Design Guidelines, and its Planning Department can tell you what applies to a specific house. Emergency drying happens inside the house, but permanent repairs that change what is visible from the street are a different matter. Ask the city before you start, and keep photos of the original materials.

Do not assume a particular repair is allowed or not allowed.

First steps

Close the supply valve if it is within safe reach. Do not scrape or pry at old plaster, and keep clear of a ceiling that is dripping. Photograph the damage along with original trim and finishes, then call for help.

Flood and storm context

No FEMA designation has been verified for the Lamar districts as a group, and this page does not claim they have a flood history. The city offers a floodplain lookup by address.

What a visit involves

A crew removes standing water, measures moisture in floors, walls and ceilings and chooses a drying plan. The structural drying page explains how readings guide the work. Photos and readings can help with an insurance claim and with preservation questions. Coverage depends on your policy.

Calling from the historic districts

Give your street and mention that the house is in a historic district so the crew can plan around original materials. The service area page and the Oxford homepage cover everywhere else.

Common questions

Which streets are in the Lamar historic districts?

The city's draft interactive map shows the boundaries. Check it or ask the City Planning Department before assuming an address is inside.

Do I need approval to dry my house?

Emergency drying is done inside the house. Permanent repairs that change what is visible from the street may involve design review, so ask the city before you start.

Is the area in a FEMA flood zone?

No single FEMA zone is confirmed for the districts. The city floodplain lookup works by address.

Can old plaster be saved after a leak?

Sometimes. A moisture reading and a close look decide, and a crew should check before cutting anything out.
