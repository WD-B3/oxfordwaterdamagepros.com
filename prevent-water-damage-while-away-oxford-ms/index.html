How to Prevent Water Damage While You Are Away

A leak that runs for a week in an empty house does far more damage than the same leak caught in an hour. A short checklist reduces the risk.

By the Oxford Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-11-10

Why empty homes are at risk

A leak at home gets noticed. A leak while you are away can run for days. By the time someone returns, water has soaked flooring, drywall and insulation, and the damage is much larger than it would have been. Insurers also look closely at long-term water loss, because policies often treat sudden leaks and long-term ones differently.

The steps below cost little and take about an hour. They are worth doing before any trip longer than a few days, and before a house sits empty between tenants or over a break.

Close the main valve

The single most effective step is shutting off the water at the main. If no water enters, no pipe can leak. Many people close it before a long trip. Open a faucet at the lowest level to drain the pipes, and leave one tap open slightly if you will be gone in cold weather and the plumber advises it. The burst pipe page explains what happens when a line fails.

If you cannot close the main, close the valves on the washing machine and the other appliances that use water.

Handle the water heater

A leaking water heater can empty a tank into a utility room. For a long trip, turn the heater to vacation mode or turn off the power or gas, depending on the advice for your model. If the main is off, the tank will not refill. The water heater page covers what happens when a tank leaks.

Check hoses and connections

Look at washing machine hoses for cracks and bulges. Replace old rubber hoses with braided stainless ones. Check the dishwasher line, the ice maker line and the supply lines under sinks. These are the most common sources of leaks that start while nobody is home.

Air conditioner and drains

Check the condensate drain on your air conditioner. A clogged drain can overflow into a ceiling or closet. Make sure the pan is clean and the line drains freely. The AC leak page explains how this happens. If you are leaving for an extended time, set the thermostat to a reasonable temperature rather than turning the system off in humid weather.

Look at the outside

Clean gutters so water flows away from the foundation. Make sure downspouts discharge a few feet from the house. Check the roof for loose or missing shingles. Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses and turn off irrigation systems.

Install leak sensors

Small battery-powered leak sensors sit on the floor and sound an alarm when water touches them. Some send alerts to your phone. Place them under sinks, beside the water heater, behind the washing machine and near the air conditioner. A sensor will not stop a leak, but it can tell you about one early.

Smart shutoff valves

Smart valves attach to the main line and close automatically when they detect unusual flow. They cost more than sensors and work best when professionally installed. They are worth considering if you travel often.

Homes that will sit for a season

Second homes and rentals between tenants need more than a short checklist. Schedule a walkthrough every week or two, run each faucet and toilet briefly to keep traps filled, and check ceilings and cabinets under sinks. A home that is empty for months should have the main valve closed and the water heater off unless someone is checking it regularly.

What to do the day you return

Walk through the house before you unpack. Look at ceilings, floors and cabinets, smell for mustiness and listen for running water. Check the meter before you reopen the main, then open the valve slowly and check again. If anything looks wrong, close the valve and call. Finding a problem on day one is much cheaper than finding it on day ten.

Ask someone to check in

Give a neighbor, friend or property manager a key and the location of the main valve. Ask them to look around every few days. A person who notices a stain or a sound can act before the damage spreads.

After storms

If a severe storm passes while you are away, ask your contact to check ceilings, attic hatches and the lowest floor. Photograph anything wet and call for help the same day.

Student housing and rentals

If you rent out a property or leave an apartment over a break, include the main valve location in your tenant handoff and ask tenants to report leaks in writing. Landlords should do a walkthrough between tenants.

Semester breaks and holidays in Oxford

Oxford empties out around University of Mississippi breaks, and many rentals and second homes sit unoccupied for weeks. That is when a small supply line leak does the most damage. If you manage a property in Oxford, make a break checklist and ask Oxford Utilities if you notice unusual usage. For permits on plumbing upgrades, contact the City of Oxford Building Department at 107 Courthouse Square.

Questions about this topic

Should I turn off the water when I travel?

For longer trips, yes. Closing the main valve removes the main risk from supply lines.

Do leak sensors work?

They alert you to water on the floor. They do not stop it, but early warning can reduce damage.

Is a smart valve worth it?

It can be for frequent travelers or vacation homes. Ask a plumber about installation.

What about the freezer or fridge?

Water lines to an ice maker can leak. Close the line valve if you will be away for long.

Who should check my home?

A trusted person who knows where the main valve is and has permission to enter.
