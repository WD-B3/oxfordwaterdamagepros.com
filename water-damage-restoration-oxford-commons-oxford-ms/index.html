Water Damage Restoration in Oxford Commons, Oxford, MS

Oxford Commons mixes single-family homes with offices and other commercial uses. Residential and business water damage follow different steps.

About Oxford Commons

Oxford Commons is a mixed-use development in Oxford. The city's Vision 2037 plan says it was first approved as a planned unit development in 2005 and annexed into the city in 2006. It adjoins residential subdivisions such as The Heights and The Preserve and a planned commercial area called The Summit.

Local news coverage in 2015 described about 500 acres with single-family homes, a professional office park, an assisted living facility and a hotel. Those details come from that coverage and may have changed since.

A neighborhood with two kinds of calls

A mixed-use area produces two types of water damage calls. Homes have the usual household failures. Offices and other commercial buildings have larger floor areas, shared plumbing and tenants who need to reopen.

- Household pipe, heater and appliance failures follow the steps on the burst pipe page and the appliance leak page

- Business losses are covered on the commercial water damage page

- Contaminated water from a drain is covered on the sewage backup page

If you own or manage a business here

Water in a commercial space raises questions a home does not. Who is the tenant, who owns the structure and who holds the insurance? Photograph the damage, close the nearest valve and notify the property manager or landlord in writing. Protect inventory and records if it is safe to move them. Do not enter a space with water near electrical equipment.

Ask your insurer about business interruption coverage and what documents it needs.

If you live here

At a house, the first hour is about the source. Close the valve, keep family members out of anything that backed up from a drain and take photos. Remove standing water only if the area is clear of electrical hazards. Wet carpet pad and soft drywall often need to come out, and a moisture meter shows how far the water traveled.

Flood and storm context

Flood-zone findings for Oxford Commons as a whole are unverified. Part of the development was described in 2015 as including county land to the south, so jurisdiction can vary by address. The city and county each maintain floodplain information, and the city engineering office can help with a specific property.

What a visit involves

A crew removes standing water, maps moisture and sets up air movers and dehumidifiers. The water extraction page covers the first stage. Photos, readings and an itemized scope can be shared with an insurer. Coverage depends on the policy and the cause of the loss.

Calling from Oxford Commons

Say whether the loss is at a home or a business when you call, and give the street. Other areas are listed on the service area page, and the Oxford homepage explains the full process.

Common questions

Is Oxford Commons inside the city of Oxford?

The city's planning documents say the development was annexed in 2006, but 2015 coverage noted some county land to the south. Check your own address with the city.

Is Oxford Commons in a FEMA flood zone?

A neighborhood-wide FEMA zone is not confirmed. Look up your address with the city or county floodplain tools.

Do you handle business water damage here?

Yes. Commercial losses are described on the commercial water damage page, and the dispatch line can explain what a visit involves.

What should a property manager do first?

Close the nearest valve, photograph the damage and notify the owner and insurer in writing. Move inventory and records only if it is safe.
