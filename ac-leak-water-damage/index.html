AC Leak Water Damage Repair in Oxford, MS

A clogged condensate line or a dripping air handler can wet ceilings and floors for weeks. Find, dry and repair AC leak damage.

☎ Call (662) 757-1011

An air conditioner that leaks inside the house is making water every day it runs, and AC leak water damage repair in Oxford, MS starts by turning the system off at the thermostat. Cooling pulls moisture from the air, and that moisture, called condensate, is supposed to drain away through a line. When the line clogs or the drain pan overflows, the water goes into ceilings, walls and floors.

A cooling system can remove several gallons of water from the air on a humid day, and a house in the humid months can make that much every day. That volume is enough to soak insulation, drywall and a wood subfloor when the drain backs up.

People often describe this as a condensation line backing up, a closet ceiling that has a wet spot, or hardwood that starts to cup near an air handler.

Signs of an AC leak

- A wet spot or stain on a ceiling near a vent or in a closet that houses the air handler

- Water in the drain pan under an indoor unit

- A musty smell near the air handler

- Dripping from a wall unit onto the floor

- Cupping or darkening of floor boards beside a unit

The leak is often slow and steady rather than sudden, and a house can be wet for weeks before anyone notices. Because the damage builds slowly, a same-day inspection is the usual response, and an emergency crew is available when the damage is spreading.

Why condensate lines clog

Condensate drains through a small diameter pipe that is easily blocked by algae, dust and debris. A float switch should shut the system off before the pan overflows, but the switch can fail or be missing. Attic air handlers are the worst case because the water goes into the ceiling below. A unit in a closet drains into the wall base and flooring.

What to do while you wait

Turn the system off at the thermostat and at the breaker if you can reach it safely. Set a bucket or towels under the drip. Move stored items out of the closet or off the floor near the air handler. Photograph the unit, the pan and the wet area.

How a crew repairs AC leak damage

The crew finds the extent of the water with moisture meters and thermal imaging, because condensate often travels along framing before it shows. Wet insulation is removed and wet drywall is cut out. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the cavity. The HVAC technician clears the line, tests the float switch and repairs the unit.

Restoration crews do not repair air conditioners. We coordinate so drying starts as soon as the HVAC tech has closed the source. For ceiling damage below an attic unit, the ceiling water damage page explains the repair.

Hardwood and AC condensate

Condensate lines that back up in a closet often wet the floor beside the unit. Hardwood cups when its bottom surface takes on more moisture than the top. If you see cupping beside an air handler, call before the boards are sanded or patched. Our hardwood floor water damage repair page explains how that is dried.

Questions Oxford homeowners ask

Do you handle AC leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Oxford?

Yes, when the damage is spreading. Dispatch is answered 24/7 and the earliest open crew is sent, often the same day. We do not promise an arrival time.

Can I keep running the AC while it drips?

Better not. Running a clogged system keeps making water. Turn it off until an HVAC technician clears the line.

Who fixes the AC itself?

An HVAC technician. We dry and repair the damage and coordinate with the technician.

Is AC leak damage covered by insurance?

It depends on the policy and on whether the leak was sudden or gradual. Ask your insurer.

How do I prevent condensate backups?

Have the line cleared and a float switch tested at regular service visits, and check the drain pan when you change filters.

Ask about the industry standard

Professional water damage work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. When you compare companies for ac leak water damage work, ask whether the crew works to it and how moisture readings will be recorded.
