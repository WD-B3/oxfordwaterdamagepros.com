When water is spreading in Taylor

Taylor is a small town in the hills of north Mississippi, with 355 residents in the 2020 census, and it sits in Lafayette County about ten miles southwest of Oxford by road. When a pipe bursts or a storm pushes water into a house here, the first call goes to the same 24/7 dispatch line that serves Oxford.

Dispatch asks where the water started, tells you what to shut off, and arranges a crew from our Oxford base. Because Taylor is outside the city, we never promise an arrival time, but we tell you plainly what is reachable and when.

For most Taylor jobs the first question is water extraction. If water is standing on a floor, water extraction has to come before any drying equipment goes in. The second question is the source. A supply line, a water heater and a washing machine hose each fail differently, and the shut-off steps are different too.

Who provides what in Taylor

Knowing who to call about the building and its services saves time during a loss. Taylor Water Association provides water service in town, so a broken service line before the meter is their concern and a broken line after the meter is yours. Northeast Mississippi Electric Power Association provides electricity. If water has reached outlets or the panel, call the power association or 911 before you touch anything.

Town Hall is at 12 Main St., Taylor, MS 38673, and the number is (662) 236-7551. Town Hall is the right starting point for questions about permits inside town limits. Properties outside the town boundary fall under Lafayette County, so ask the county's building and planning department there.

Flood Zone A and what it means for repairs

The Town of Taylor publishes a FEMA flood map and a flood damage prevention ordinance, number 2025-1. The town states that some areas within town limits are in FEMA Flood Zone A, and that the town floodplain administrator reviews planned development in that zone through a floodplain development permit. The practical point is simple.

If your house or lot is in a mapped flood area and you plan to rebuild, elevate or add to the structure after a loss, ask Town Hall about the permit before the work starts. Check the FEMA flood map for your address to see where you stand.

Flood zones also change the insurance conversation. Standard homeowners coverage typically leaves out rising water, and it is a separate flood policy that normally responds. Read your policy and ask your insurer, and keep your photos and moisture records either way. For rising water in particular, see our flood damage cleanup page.

Hill country water and where it goes

Taylor lies in the hills of north Mississippi, and water follows the slope. After heavy rain, look at where your lot drains. Water that runs toward a foundation, a low door sill or a crawl space vent finds its way inside faster than water that runs past. If you find damp insulation or a musty subfloor after a storm, start with the crawl space water damage page.

Taylor Grocery, the catfish restaurant that began as a grocery store, and the other businesses on Main Street are a reminder that commercial water loss happens here too. A restaurant or shop owner should photograph equipment, stock and finishes before cleanup and ask the insurer what proof of loss is required. Our commercial water damage page explains how business losses are handled.

Before we arrive in Taylor

CARDS

Stop the source :: Close the fixture valve or the main water valve. If you cannot find the main, call and ask the dispatcher to talk you through it.

Make the area safe :: Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Should water be reaching outlets or the panel, stay away and phone the power association or 911.

Lift what you can :: Move rugs, small furniture and electronics off wet floors. Leave heavy furniture where it is if moving it would risk injury.

Document and call :: Take photos and short video before you move anything, keep children and pets out of any water that came from a drain, then call the 24/7 line.

What a Taylor job usually involves

Most calls from Taylor follow one of three patterns. A plumbing failure puts clean water into a kitchen, bathroom or utility room, and the work is extraction, drying the wall cavities and replacing wet drywall. A storm leak lets rain in through the roof or around windows, and the work is finding the entry point, drying insulation and ceilings, and repairing finishes.

A rising-water event is the most serious, because floodwater is treated as contaminated and porous materials usually have to come out. Each of those follows the same order. Stop the source, remove the water, dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers, record moisture readings, then rebuild.

Before work starts you get a written scope and a clear explanation of how the price is built. If your insurer needs the records, ask for the photos, the readings and the itemized scope when the job finishes.

Who do I call about a water main problem in Taylor?

Taylor Water Association provides water service in town. A broken line before the meter is a utility matter, and a broken line after the meter is the homeowner's. Close the valve at your house if you can reach it safely, then call us for the cleanup.

Does my Taylor house need a permit to repair water damage?

Permit rules vary by the type of work and by whether the property is inside town limits. Town Hall at (662) 236-7551 can tell you what applies inside Taylor, and the county building and planning department handles properties outside town limits. If your lot is in a mapped flood area, ask about the floodplain development permit before rebuilding.

Is flood damage covered by homeowners insurance in Taylor?

Usually not. Standard homeowners policies typically exclude rising water, and a separate flood policy responds to it. Read your policy and ask your insurer, then document the damage with dated photos and moisture readings before cleanup.
