Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak: Save It or Toss It?

Soft furniture soaks up water and keeps it. The type of water and how long it sat decide what is worth saving.

By the Oxford Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-11-10

Why soft furniture is hard to dry

Mattresses and sofas are made of foam, batting and fabric, all of which hold water deep inside. The surface may feel nearly dry while the core is still wet. That hidden moisture is what causes odor and mold growth later, which is why the decision about whether to keep a wet piece depends on more than how it looks.

Three questions settle most cases. What kind of water was it, how long did it sit and what is the item made of?

Start with the water type

Clean water from a broken supply line or a rainwater leak is the easiest case. Water from a washing machine, dishwasher or aquarium is dirtier. Water from a drain, a toilet or a flood is the worst, and soft furniture that touches it is nearly always discarded.

The sewage backup page explains why. Porous materials cannot be reliably cleaned after contact with sewage or floodwater, and no one wants a contaminated mattress in the bedroom.

What to do in the first hour

1. Stop the source of the water.

2. Move the item out of the wet area if it is safe to lift.

3. Take cushions off the frame and stand them on edge to drain.

4. Blot, do not rub. Use towels to press out water.

5. Open windows or run a dehumidifier to move air.

6. Photograph the item before cleaning in case you need to document a claim.

Do not put a wet mattress in direct sun for hours, because heat can warp foam. Do not use a household steam cleaner on deep water, because it pushes moisture further in.

Mattresses

Memory foam and innerspring mattresses with clean-water exposure may be saved if they are dried promptly. The drying has to reach the core. A surface spill that was blotted quickly is a different case from a mattress sitting in an inch of water overnight. If the mattress has been wet for more than a day or two, or if it smells, plan to replace it.

Mattress warranties often exclude water damage, so check yours before you assume a manufacturer will replace it.

Sofas and upholstered chairs

A sofa has a wood or metal frame, springs, foam and fabric. The frame and springs can usually be dried and cleaned. Foam and batting are the problem. Cushions that were wet with clean water and dried quickly can sometimes be saved by a professional cleaner. Cushions that stayed wet or smelled musty should go.

Leather and microfiber react differently. Leather may stain or stiffen as it dries, and microfiber can ring. A cleaner who specializes in upholstery can tell you which is which.

Pillows, rugs and cushions

Pillows and small cushions that were clean-water wet can be washed and dried hot. Rugs depend on the backing. Wool rugs dry and clean well in many cases, and synthetic rugs with jute backing may release dye. Anything that touched sewage is discarded.

What a professional can do that you cannot

Professional drying uses meters to find water deep in the cushion and extraction tools that pull water out of foam. For a prized or expensive piece, an upholstery cleaner may be a better call than a restoration crew. For the room, though, the crew's job is to dry the floor, the walls and the air, and that is what keeps a salvaged piece from getting wet again.

Odor is a signal

A musty smell means moisture is still inside. If the smell comes back after drying, the item is not truly dry. Further cleaning may not fix it.

Drying a mattress that can be saved

Stand the mattress on its side against a wall with air on both faces, in a room with a running dehumidifier. Turn it every few hours. Press a clean towel into the core to see if it picks up moisture. Expect this to take days, not hours, and expect to replace it if a musty smell appears. A mattress protector, once the item is dry, helps limit future spills.

Document before you discard

Take photos of the item, the label if there is one, and the wet area. Note the date and the cause. Your insurer may want an inventory of damaged belongings, and it is easier to do that before the item leaves the house.

Oxford homes, rentals and storage

Many Oxford households include rented apartments and student housing, and a leak can damage furniture that belongs to the tenant while the structure belongs to the landlord. Report the leak in writing and keep photos of everything that was wet. If your belongings were damaged, renters insurance, if you have it, may apply. Ask your insurer what it covers.

Questions about this topic

Can a wet mattress be dried at home?

If the water was clean and it was caught quickly, blotting, airflow and a dehumidifier help. A mattress with deep water or odor is usually replaced.

Is it safe to keep furniture that touched sewage?

No. Porous items that contacted sewage or floodwater are generally discarded.

Will a hair dryer help?

It dries the surface and can push moisture deeper. Airflow and dehumidification work better.

Does insurance cover furniture?

Homeowners and renters policies may cover contents damage from a sudden leak. Ask your insurer and document the items.

How do I know if a cushion is dry?

Press the center and smell it. A cold, heavy cushion or a musty smell means it is still wet.
