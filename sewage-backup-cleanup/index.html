Do not flush toilets or run water, because anything that goes down the drain may come back up.

Raw sewage coming up the drain: what to do first

Homeowners describe this in plain terms: the drain downstairs backed up and it is nothing but raw sewage, or a toilet that overflows and will not stop. That is Category 3 water, the most contaminated category in the industry standard. It carries bacteria and viruses and should never be cleaned with a household mop.

  1. Stop using water in the house. Do not flush or run faucets.
  2. Keep children, pets and anyone with health concerns out of the area.
  3. If water has reached outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out and call the utility or 911 first.
  4. Do not walk through the water or track it into other rooms.
  5. Photograph the area from a dry spot and call the 24/7 line.

The dispatch line is open overnight, because backups do not wait for business hours. Ask about a same-day visit, which depends on crew availability.

Why sewage backs up

A backup comes from a blockage in the main sewer line from the house, a collapsed line, roots entering a joint, or a city sewer that is overloaded during heavy rain. The cause decides who is responsible. A clog in the line on your property is yours to clear. A problem in the city main belongs to the sewer utility, and Oxford Utilities provides sewer service for the city.

Call a plumber or the utility to find the blockage, and call us for the cleanup.

What the crew does

The crew wears protective gear. Standing sewage is pumped out first and the area is contained so contamination does not spread. Porous materials that touched sewage, such as carpet, pad, drywall, insulation and upholstered furniture, are removed and bagged. Surfaces that can be cleaned receive disinfectant. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the remaining structure, and odor control follows.

Do not try to dry and keep carpet that contacted sewage. The risk is not worth the saving, and most insurers and standards treat it as a loss.

What can be saved

Concrete, tile, vinyl and framing can usually be cleaned and disinfected. Drywall below the water line, carpet, pad and insulation are removed. Contents that absorbed sewage, such as upholstered furniture and mattresses, are discarded, while hard goods can often be cleaned.

Safety, health and the second call

If you or anyone in the house is ill, speak with a physician about health questions. This page does not diagnose or advise on illness. Wash hands carefully after any contact and do not eat or drink in the affected area.

Questions Oxford homeowners ask

Do you handle sewage backup emergencies at night or on weekends in Oxford?

Yes. The dispatch line is answered 24/7 and sewage calls are treated as urgent. Keep people out of the water while you call. A same-day visit is possible when schedules allow, and we do not promise an exact arrival time.

Is sewage water dangerous?

It can carry bacteria and viruses, and it should be handled by crews with protective gear. Ask a physician about any health concern.

Who clears the blockage?

A plumber or the sewer utility. We clean up the contaminated area and dry the structure.

Does insurance cover a sewer backup?

Often not without a specific endorsement. Ask your insurer.

Can I clean up a small backup myself?

Even small amounts of sewage carry health risks, so protective gear and disinfectants are needed. When in doubt, call.

Ask about the industry standard

Professional water damage work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. When you compare companies for sewage backup cleanup work, ask whether the crew works to it and how moisture readings will be recorded.
