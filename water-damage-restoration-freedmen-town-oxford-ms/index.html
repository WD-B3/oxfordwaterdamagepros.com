Water Damage Restoration in Freedmen Town, Oxford, MS

Freedmen Town is a long-established Oxford neighborhood with many longtime homeowners. Here is how water damage is handled and how to protect what matters.

About Freedmen Town

Freedmen Town grew after the Civil War, when formerly enslaved people moved into Oxford and built homes, churches and schools. Local historian Jack Mayfield, quoted by the Oxford Eagle, describes the neighborhood as lying between North Seventh and Fifth Streets, and from South Price Street to Jackson Avenue. Martin Luther King Jr.

Street runs through it, and the Burns-Belfry Museum and Multicultural Center, on Molly Barr Road at Washington Avenue, preserves part of its history. Exact boundaries are not officially fixed, so use the streets above as a guide.

The Oxford Eagle reports that the area remains residential, with many longtime residents. That matters for restoration because homes held by one family for decades often hold things that cannot be replaced.

Protecting what cannot be replaced

When water gets into a home with family photographs, papers and keepsakes, the first hour is about protecting them. Move them to a dry room if it is safe, and lift them off wet floors. Paper and photographs can be frozen to stop further damage until they can be dried properly. Photograph the items and the room before you move them, in case an insurer asks for proof of loss.

Where leaks tend to start in long-held homes

Houses that have stood for a long time have seen many repairs. Supply lines, water heaters and drain lines get replaced one at a time, and a joint that was fine for years can fail without warning. Nothing here says older houses leak more often. It says that a failure can come from any one of several repairs made at different times.

- A failed pipe, covered under burst pipe water damage

- A stained or sagging ceiling, covered under ceiling water damage repair

- Damp walls and baseboards, covered under wall and drywall repair

- Wood floors that cup after a leak, covered under hardwood floor repair

First steps

Shut the supply valve only if you can reach it safely. Drain or sewer water is a health risk, so children and pets stay out of it. Leave outlets and the panel alone when water is nearby, and call a professional for anything larger than a spill.

What happens during a visit

A crew removes standing water and checks the floor and walls with a moisture meter. Materials that cannot dry, such as soaked pad and soft drywall, are removed. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the rest. The water extraction page covers the first stage in detail. You will hear the scope and the price before work begins.

Common questions

Where is Freedmen Town in Oxford?

Local historian Jack Mayfield places it between North Seventh and Fifth Streets, and from South Price Street to Jackson Avenue. Boundaries are not officially fixed.

Is Freedmen Town in a flood zone?

No neighborhood-wide FEMA finding is verified here. Zones are mapped property by property, so use the city floodplain lookup for your address.

How do I protect family photographs after a leak?

Lift them off wet surfaces, photograph them where they sit and keep them out of sealed bags at room temperature. Freezing wet photographs and papers stops damage until they can be dried.

Does a longtime owner need to do anything different with insurance?

Read the policy and ask the insurer what documents it wants. Dated photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope are the records most often requested.
