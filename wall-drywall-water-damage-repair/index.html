Wall and Drywall Water Damage Repair in Oxford, MS

Drywall wicks water upward and hides damage inside the wall. Dry, cut out and rebuild walls, baseboards and trim.

☎ Call (662) 757-1011

Wall and drywall water damage repair in Oxford, MS starts inside the wall, not on its surface. Drywall is gypsum sandwiched between paper, and both parts absorb water. It wicks upward like a paper towel, so a wall that was wet only at the base can show damage a foot or more above the floor. Behind the paper are insulation, framing, wiring and plumbing that may also be wet.

People describe walls in different ways: water damage on baseboards, a hole forming in the kitchen wall, a soft spot, a stain at the corner of the wall and the ceiling. All of them point to moisture in the wall cavity, and finding how much is the first job.

Signs of wall water damage

- Bubbling, peeling or discolored paint

- Drywall that feels soft, crumbly or cold

- Baseboards that swell or pull away from the wall

- Stains that return after painting

- A bulge or a crack along a seam

- A musty smell along an exterior wall

What to do first

1. Stop the water source.

2. Move furniture away from the wall and remove anything resting on the baseboard.

3. Do not drill into a wall near wiring if you are unsure where it runs.

4. Photograph the wall and the stain before anything is changed.

5. Call for a moisture inspection.

Water behind a wall often comes from a plumbing line, so it is worth treating as active until proven otherwise. Dispatch is open 24/7, and a same-day visit is possible when schedules allow.

How a crew checks a wall

The crew scans the wall with a thermal camera to find cool patches where water is evaporating. A moisture meter reads the drywall, the baseboard and the framing. Small test holes may be made in hidden spots. The readings show how high the water traveled and whether the cavity is still wet.

Cutting out or drying in place

Drywall that is only slightly damp and sound can sometimes be dried in place with air pushed into the cavity through small holes. Drywall that is saturated, soft or crumbly is cut out. A common practice is to cut at a straight line some inches above the highest wet reading, which makes the patch easier to finish. Wet insulation comes out.

Framing is dried and checked before new drywall goes up.

Contaminated water changes that decision. Drywall wetted by floodwater or sewage is removed even if it looks fine.

Baseboards, trim and paint

Baseboards swell and separate from the wall when wet. Wood trim can be dried and reused in some cases, and MDF trim often cannot. After the wall is dry and repaired, the crew finishes and paints it. Paint on drywall that is still damp bubbles again, which is why readings are taken before any finishing.

Walls that were wet from above

Water that ran down a wall from a ceiling leak, a roof leak or a bathroom upstairs is covered by the ceiling water damage page as well. A leak from a supply line inside the wall is covered under burst pipe water damage, and a wall near a sink is also a kitchen water damage matter.

Questions Oxford homeowners ask

Can I paint over a water stain?

Only after the wall is confirmed dry and the source is fixed. Painting a damp wall hides the damage and lets it continue.

Do I need to cut the drywall out?

If it is soft, crumbly, bulging or wet from contaminated water, yes. If it is firm and slightly damp, it may dry in place.

How do I know if water is inside the wall?

Stains, bubbles, baseboard swelling and a musty smell are signs. A moisture meter and thermal camera confirm it.

How long do walls take to dry?

Several days for clean water caught early, and longer for thick or enclosed walls.

Is wall damage covered by insurance?

Sudden leaks are often covered, and slow leaks inside walls often are not. Ask your insurer.

Ask about the industry standard

Professional water damage work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. When you compare companies for wall and drywall water damage work, ask whether the crew works to it and how moisture readings will be recorded.
